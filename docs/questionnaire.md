# Brand interview

The interview is a conversation, not a required online form.
Ask up to three questions at once.
Save each useful answer before the next group.
Explain unfamiliar terms before the owner must choose.
Use catalog/questions.json for the full coverage list.

## First group

- What is your business called?
- What do you sell or do?
- Where do you serve customers?

Then ask about the main customer, the business difference, and the brand goal.
Ask for one specific customer example if the owner says “everyone.”
Keep business facts separate from assumptions.
Inspect a supplied website when your tools allow it.
Record its URL and the check date.

## Character and references

Ask for three character words, styles to avoid, and optional references.
Offer “I do not have references. Give me original ideas.”
Inspect each reference before you describe it.
Use references for principles, such as spacing or contrast.
Do not copy distinctive artwork.

## Logo structure

Ask which product or service the symbol should show.
Ask for useful photos or references, if available.
Offer relevant original subjects when references are absent.
Follow docs/design-logic.md before artwork.

Explain these choices with small visual examples:

- A wordmark uses the business name as the logo.
- A monogram uses initials as the logo.
- A symbol uses a separate visual mark.
- A combined logo places the symbol beside the name.

Ask about the main use and the smallest required size.
Ask which symmetry rule fits the brief.
Mirror symmetry reflects a shape across an axis.
Rotational symmetry repeats a shape around a center.
Optical balance adjusts the design so that it looks balanced.

Do not make every wordmark symmetrical.
Letter shapes and spaces often need optical corrections.

## Fonts and colors

Ask about required colors, the type character, and all required languages.
Ask about existing assets, required files, and the budget for paid fonts.
Show real text in each font before selection.
Include the business name, a heading, body text, numerals, and required script examples.
Suggest one heading font and one body font with clear roles.
Use one family when it serves both roles well.

## Voice and rules

Ask how the brand should speak, which rules must persist, and who approves the work.
Save exact spelling, prohibited symbols, required colors, and required uses.
Record “no preference” as an answer when the owner leaves a choice to the agent.
Do not ask the same question again unless the answer creates a specific conflict.

## Visual decision

Show seven directions with real previews.
State why each direction fits this business.
Show a symbol, name treatment, font pair, palette, and one relevant use for each direction.
The CLI board renders the business-linked geometry from the saved design plan.
It shows the subject, business connection, customer reason, and font and color reasons.
Check those reasons against the actual brief.

Allow the owner to combine parts or request a new direction.
Create one sample in the chosen direction.
Get approval for the exact sample before the full kit.

## Example conversation

Agent: “What is your business called? What do you sell or do? Where do you serve customers?”

Owner: “Fieldwork Coffee. A small cafe and coffee beans. We serve our neighborhood.”

Agent: “I saved those answers. Who is your main customer? Why should they choose you? What should this brand achieve?”

Owner: “Local adults. Calm service and a clear menu. We need cups, a sign, and a website.”

Agent: “I will compare seven directions after we define the logo, fonts, and colors. References are optional.”

This conversation is a fictional example.
