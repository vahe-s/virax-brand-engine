# Design logic before artwork

Every logo needs a visible connection to the business brief.
Save and present the design plan before artwork.
The plan records the design reasons. It does not expose private internal reasoning.

## Decision sequence

1. Confirm the product, service, audience, business difference, and required logo uses.
2. Ask which product or service the symbol should show.
3. Ask whether the owner has photos, existing artwork, or visual references.
4. Inspect supplied references and record the useful visual details.
5. Offer recognizable business subjects when references are absent.
6. Explain each subject's connection to the offer and customer needs.
7. Record the owner's permission before an abstract symbol.
8. Choose fonts, colors, and geometry that support those meanings.
9. Save the design plan and its links to the owner's answers.
10. Create seven previews from the saved concepts.
11. Help the owner choose or revise a concept.
12. Create the selected sample and test its required uses.

References are optional. Business meaning is required.
An owner can choose original ideas without a reference image.
That choice does not permit an unrelated icon.

## Coffee example

The offer is a cafe and coffee beans.
Useful subjects include a coffee bean, cup, takeaway cup, or coffee plant.
Ask which subject fits the business and its main use.
Do not select a cup automatically if the business mainly sells packaged beans.
Use the bean concept for the product when it gives a clearer connection.

The example plan contains seven coffee-related concepts.
It explains each subject, customer reason, type choice, palette choice, and symmetry rule.
It also identifies palette options that differ from the owner's saved preference.
The owner can reject those options before sample creation.

## Car example

First confirm whether the business sells cars, repairs cars, rents cars, or produces car parts.
A car silhouette can represent a dealer or rental service.
A car with a tool cue can represent repair work.
A wheel or a specific part can represent a specialist supplier.
Do not use a racing symbol for a calm family rental service without a clear reason.
Record the chosen subject before SVG creation.

## Required concept record

- The actual subject that the icon shows.
- Its connection to the confirmed offer.
- The customer reason for that subject.
- The profile answers that support it.
- A summary of inspected references, or the original-ideas choice.
- Reasons for the fonts, palette, and symmetry rule.
- The business-linked geometric source.
- Owner permission for an abstract concept, when applicable.

The engine requires these records before directions, selection, or sample creation.
The validator checks record structure and required links.
It cannot prove that an icon depicts its claimed subject or that a design reason is sound.
The agent must inspect the artwork and compare it with the brief.
Do not invent reference inspection or owner permission.

## Changes and approval

A profile change clears the design plan, selected concept, sample, and approval.
A design plan change clears the selected concept, sample, and approval.
Revise the plan before you change its geometry.
Keep the exact plan with the approved sample and exported kit.
Existing profiles need the new subject answer and a design plan before further artwork or exports.
Keep the earlier files as history.
